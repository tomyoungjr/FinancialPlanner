Your world

A 30-year plan built on one set of assumptions is linear thinking. This screen is your assessment of how the foundations could shift: your odds that each change happens inside a window of years. How it works

A personal planner that doesn't assume the next 30 years will look like the last 30. It combines TJ's Get FIREd planning model with a nonlinear layer for regime changes and structural disruptions.

Three layers

  1. The household model (from Get FIREd). Accounts by tax type (cash, brokerage, pre-tax, Roth), taxes on pay and withdrawals, the 10% penalty before 59½, required minimum distributions, spending by life stage, health costs that outpace inflation, an inheritance entered as a range and left out of the headline, and spending guardrails that report how often and how deeply you'd cut back.
  2. Policy scenarios (from Get FIREd). Switches for specific laws, each with what pays for it, run side by side through identical markets. No odds, and no effect on markets, so every difference is checkable.
  3. Your world (the nonlinear layer). The economy moves between regimes that last for years (steady growth, stagflation, deflationary bust, AI boom), each with its own returns, inflation and stock/bond correlation. Disruptions happen at your odds, can cascade, and may move markets. This is where a 60/40 portfolio with a 4% draw gets tested against a world that isn't linear.

Each simulated year

  1. Disruptions that happen this year take effect permanently.
  2. The economy lands in a regime; returns and inflation are drawn together from it.
  3. Income (pay, Social Security, UBI, part-time) comes in, taxed; spending, health care, VAT and wealth tax go out.
  4. The gap is withdrawn: cash first, then brokerage, then pre-tax and Roth (Roth before pre-tax until 59½ to avoid the penalty). Required distributions come out regardless.
  5. With guardrails on, discretionary spending is trimmed 10% whenever the withdrawal rate drifts 20% above where retirement started, and raised 5% when it drifts 20% below.

Success means every year's spending was covered through the end of the plan. The end moves out if a longevity breakthrough happens.

Principles (kept from Get FIREd)

  • It only advises. It computes, simulates and flags. Nothing in it can move money.
  • It runs at home. A single web page opened from your own computer. No server, no account, no internet needed. Your numbers stay in this browser.
  • No personal details in the code. The code holds placeholders; your numbers live in the browser and in export files that git ignores.
  • Plain math, checkable. Every number comes from ordinary code with tests. If an AI helps later, it explains results; it doesn't do the math.
  • A range, not a promise. Inheritance is left out of the headline unless you include it.

Where the two models differ, on purpose

  • Get FIREd deliberately keeps policies from moving markets and assigns no odds, to stay simple and checkable. The Scenarios screen keeps that discipline.
  • Your World deliberately does both: it lets a debt crisis force years of inflation and AI shift the odds of a boom or a bust, and it puts your odds in the open. The two screens answer different questions: "what if this law passes?" and "what do I actually believe about the next 30 years?"

Simplifications (good next steps)

  • One blended ordinary tax rate instead of brackets; no state-specific rules or Roth conversions.
  • Social Security is one household figure; no spousal or survivor detail.
  • No house, mortgage, annuity or long-term-care modeling.
  • Replaying real market history (1928 onward) is a planned alternative to the random draws.
  • Account feeds (Plaid, statements), transaction sorting and the ledger from Get FIREd are not part of this planner.
